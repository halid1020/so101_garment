\begin{abstract}
This report compares \new{state-of-the-art learning-based control policies} for
a two-armed robot that folds a garment, \new{using data collected with five
cameras: one overhead and four tactile sensors on the fingertips.} \new{We train
three behaviour-cloning policies, one vision-language-action policy and two
world action models} from the same teleoperated demonstrations. We ask three
questions. \new{How does each policy perform on the demonstrations it was
trained on, and on demonstrations it never saw? Does cropping the edges of the
tactile images help, and does it change which parts of those images the models
rely on?} And how well do the world action models predict what the cameras will
see next? \new{We find that the world action models, which also predict the
future camera images, plan their next actions most accurately on unseen
demonstrations.} Every model is much worse on unseen demonstrations than on the
ones it trained on, and cropping the tactile images does not change that. We
describe how every figure was made, so that each claim can be checked.
\end{abstract}
