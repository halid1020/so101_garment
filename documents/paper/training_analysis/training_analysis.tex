\begin{abstract}
A bimanual rig fitted with fingertip tactile cameras learns garment folding
from teleoperated demonstrations. Gradient attribution computed on training
recordings placed weight at the edges of the tactile images, including before
contact, which is consistent with light entering at the boundary of the
sensing gel rather than with contact being sensed. This report evaluates the
remedy that follows from that reading: cropping each tactile image to its
centre. Every policy is retrained with a held-out split so that behaviour can
be measured on recordings it never saw, and each cropped policy is matched to
its uncropped twin in architecture, optimisation budget and data. The
comparison reverses several conclusions that had been drawn from training
recordings alone, and two of those reverse a second time once the families are
compared over a planning horizon they share rather than over their own. We give
the provenance of every figure so that each claim can be checked, and we state
the confounds that remain open, including one that the present split cannot
separate.
\end{abstract}
