\begin{abstract}
This report compares \new{state-of-the-art learning-based control policies} for
a two-armed robot that folds a garment, \new{using data collected with five
cameras: one overhead and four tactile sensors on the fingertips.} \new{We train
three behaviour-cloning policies, one vision-language-action policy and two
world action models} from the same teleoperated demonstrations. We ask three
questions. \new{How does each policy perform on the demonstrations it was
trained on, and on demonstrations it never saw?} \bl{\gn{Does naive integration
of tactile images help to improve the policy performance?}} \bl{And how well do the
world action models predict \gn{the future observations}?} \new{We find that the world action models, which also predict the
future camera images, plan their next actions most accurately on unseen
demonstrations.} \bl{\gn{In general, every} model is much worse on unseen demonstrations than on the
ones it trained on.}
\end{abstract}
