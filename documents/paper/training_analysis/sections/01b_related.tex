\begin{gntext}
\section{\bl{Related work}}
\label{sec:related}

\bl{Integrating tactile sensing has become a popular trend in robot learning
\citep{huang2025vitac,heng2026vitacformer,zhu2025touchwild,xue2025rdp,bi2026vlatouch,lou2026dreamtac}.}
\bl{This section reviews that literature from four angles.} \dg{We first summarise the
tasks on which touch has been shown to help, and what it contributes to each
(Section~\ref{sec:rw-tasks}), and the tactile sensors these works use, including
our own (Section~\ref{sec:rw-sensors}).} \dg{We then set out the difficulties that
integrating touch into a learned policy faces (Section~\ref{sec:rw-hard}), and how
each of the three model families we compare has addressed them
(Section~\ref{sec:rw-families}).}

\subsection{\bl{Where touch has been shown to help}}
\label{sec:rw-tasks}

\dg{Touch helps most where what decides success cannot be seen by the cameras.} \dg{The
object may be hidden from them, as an egg in its tray or grapes inside a bag; it
may be held in the hand, where the fingers hide how it sits; or what matters may
be a force, which no camera measures.} \dg{Across the works we reviewed, touch
contributes in four ways; Figure~\ref{fig:rw-tasks} shows examples of the tasks.}

\bl{\textbf{Confirming and regulating a grasp.}} \bl{In 3D-ViTac's egg-steaming task,
touch confirms that an egg is held securely, sets the grip force on the lid
handle and confirms that the hand has cleared the other eggs; in its
fruit-preparation task, performed inside an opaque bag, it confirms that grapes
are held and keeps the grip from crushing them \citep{huang2025vitac}.} \bl{FARM
picks grapes without crushing them by predicting a target grip force and
regulating it in closed loop \citep{helmut2026farm}.} \bl{Reactive Diffusion Policy
lifts a paper cup between two arms without squeezing it, applying an outward
correction once contact is made; vision alone scored 0 on that task and the
tactile policy 0.70 \citep{xue2025rdp}.}

\dg{\textbf{Tracking an object or a tool inside the hand.}} \dg{In 3D-ViTac's hex-key
task, one hand picks up an Allen key by its tail, the key slips a little in the
grasp, and the two hands must then turn it until it sits perpendicular to the
fingers before inserting it into a hole \citep{huang2025vitac}.} \dg{The fingers hide
from the cameras where the key sits and how it is tilted between them; touch
shows it.} \dg{Without touch the policy usually failed to turn the key into place, and
the whole task succeeded in 0.65 of trials, against 0.90 with touch. 3D-ViTac
also follows how a spoon rotates passively in the hand while scooping.}
\bl{Touch in the Wild reorients a transparent test
tube against the edge of a rack, where vision misjudged the tube's orientation
and was distracted by the colour of its cork; touch raised success from 0.25 to
0.85 \citep{zhu2025touchwild}.} \dg{A tool doubles the problem, since it touches the
hand at one end and the world at the other.} \dg{Shirai et al.\ estimate the pose of a
grasped tool and of the object it pushes from GelSlim images, and detect the
tool's slip from the motion of the gel's markers, so that a controller can pivot
objects with the tool in closed loop \citep{shirai2023tool}.} \dg{Aoyama et al.\ teach
surface following with grasped tools from a few human demonstrations, combining
tactile and proximity sensing to tell the hand--tool contact from the
tool--surface one \citep{aoyama2025tool}.} \dg{In PolyTouch's egg serving, where one arm
scoops a toy fried egg with a spatula, adding touch and contact audio raised
success from 66 to 100\,\% \citep{zhao2025polytouch}.}

\bl{\textbf{Keeping a contact force.}} \bl{Wiping, peeling and erasing need a steady
force against a surface the camera cannot measure.} \bl{Reactive Diffusion Policy
corrects a peeler upwards when its contact force spikes, and adapts force and
rotation while wiping a curved vase \citep{xue2025rdp}; Touch in the Wild keeps
an eraser pressed to a whiteboard, where vision-only policies pressed too
lightly and left marks \citep{zhu2025touchwild}; and it detects when a pipette
bulb is fully squeezed, a phase vision-only policies skipped
\citep{zhu2025touchwild}.} \dg{ViTacFormer's dexterous hands wipe ink off a vase by
following its contour with their fingertips, which they did in 9 of 10 trials,
against 3 for ACT without touch \citep{heng2026vitacformer}.}

\bl{\textbf{Judging a material.}} \bl{VLA-Touch chooses the smoother of two sponges or
the softer of two mangoes from tactile descriptions of roughness and hardness
\citep{bi2026vlatouch}.}

\dg{3D-ViTac also shows that touch can make up for missing cameras
\citep{huang2025vitac}.} \dg{In its egg-steaming task, the fraction of successful
trials rose from 0.50 to 0.80 when touch was added to a policy with one camera,
and from 0.55 to 0.85 when it was added to a policy with three cameras: the same
gain of 0.30 in both cases.} \dg{With touch, the one-camera policy (0.80) did better
than the three-camera policy without it (0.55).} \bl{Touch was compensating for what the
cameras could not see.}

\bl{\textbf{Garments, our task, are less studied, and harder.}} \bl{A garment is thin and
compliant, and a parallel gripper must pinch a single layer of cloth that lies
flat on the table, a grasp in which the fingertips hardly see the cloth before
they close on it.} \bl{A fold may also require grasping several layers at once, or a
chosen number of them, and a two-armed fold grasps different keypoints of the
garment at the same time.} \bl{Whether a grasp holds one layer, two, or none is
exactly the kind of state touch should reveal and the overhead camera cannot.}
\dg{A few works sense it.} \dg{Tirumala et al.\ put a ReSkin magnetic skin on one
fingertip and train a classifier that tells whether the gripper holds one or two
layers of a cloth stack; the robot grasps again at a slightly different height
until the classifier agrees \citep{tirumala2022cloth}.} \dg{Picking one layer, it
succeeded in 10 of 10 trials on the towel it was trained on, against 8 for the
same method with an image classifier, and in 8 and 7 of 10 on two unseen cloths,
against 3 and 2; two layers were harder, at 4 and 1 of 10 on the unseen cloths.}
\dg{Sunil et al.\ use GelSight fingertips to classify whether a grasp holds a cloth
edge, a corner, a fold, all fabric or nothing, and to estimate the edge's position
and angle within the grasp, so that the robot can slide along the edge to the next
corner and unfold a towel \citep{sunil2022cloth}; their later system grasps
crumpled and hanging garments for folding and hanging, with the same kind of
tactile classifier checking each grasp \citep{sunil2025inair}.} \dg{These works use
touch to check and correct a grasp inside a pipeline designed by hand.} \dg{Whether
naive tactile input helps a policy learned end to end fold a garment, and in
which model family, is the question this report starts to answer.}

\dg{Figure~\ref{fig:sensors-rw} shows examples of the four kinds of sensor these works
use, and what each one records.}
\bl{\emph{Vision-based gel sensors} film the inside of a soft elastomer pad with a
small camera, so contact appears as a change in its shading or in the motion of
markers printed on it; GelSight \citep{gelsight} and DIGIT \citep{digit} are the
common designs.} \bl{They resolve the shape of a contact finely, but at camera rates
(25--30\,Hz) and inside a bulky fingertip.} \bl{\emph{Piezoresistive arrays} sandwich
a pressure-sensitive film between two orthogonal sets of electrodes, so each
crossing is a taxel whose resistance falls under load. 3D-ViTac's pads have
$16\times16$ taxels of about 3\,mm\textsuperscript{2} each, are under 1\,mm thick,
read at up to 32\,Hz and cost about \$20 \citep{huang2025vitac}; Touch in the Wild
uses $12\times32$ pads with 2\,mm taxels at 23\,Hz \citep{zhu2025touchwild}.} \bl{They
are thin and cheap but measure only normal pressure, at millimetre resolution.}
\bl{\emph{Multimodal fingertips} add channels a camera cannot provide.} \bl{PolyTouch
combines a camera-based gel, a contact microphone and a side window through
which the same camera sees the scene, and lasts at least twenty times longer
than commercial sensors \citep{zhao2025polytouch}; the Digit~360 fingertip behind
Sparsh-X records a tactile image, contact audio, acceleration and pressure
\citep{higuera2025sparshx}; and TacThru's transparent gel lets one camera see the
contact and the scene beyond it \citep{li2026tacthru}.} \bl{\emph{Magnetic skins}
embed magnetised particles in an elastomer over magnetometers, which read its
deformation as a change in field; ReSkin's skins are thin, cheap and replaceable
\citep{bhirangi2021reskin}.} \bl{Some policies read force instead of a tactile image:
Reactive Diffusion Policy also runs on the arm's joint torques
\citep{xue2025rdp}.}

\bl{\textbf{Our sensors.}} \bl{Our rig carries four vision-based gel sensors, one on each
fingertip.} \bl{In use they have two drawbacks.} \bl{The soft gel wears quickly, and the
four sensors do not see alike: their gels have worn differently and sit at
slightly different angles, so the same contact looks different on each finger,
and one sensor shifted partway through our recording session
(Section~\ref{sec:recordings}).} \bl{We therefore plan to replace the soft elastomer
with a frosted acrylic sheet viewed by the same camera from behind: a rigid
surface that does not wear like a gel and can be made identically for every
finger.} \bl{We found no learned-manipulation work using such a sensor.} \bl{The closest
designs we found let an object touch a rigid acrylic plate directly and detect
the contact optically, which gives contact position and area but no imprint of
deformation \citep{shimonomura2019review}, or keep a soft gel on the contact
side of a rigid acrylic window, so that only light scattered at true contacts
reaches the camera \citep{lin2025lighttact}.} \bl{A frosted sheet should also scatter
light from outside and lower contrast, so its housing will need to shield it.}
\bl{Another route to durability is a protective film over the gel: a textured film
outlasted commercial gels by more than two orders of magnitude in accelerated
tests \citep{cottrell2026durable}.}

\subsection{\bl{What makes touch hard to learn from}}
\label{sec:rw-hard}

\bl{Integrating tactile sensing into robot learning faces four major difficulties.}

\bl{\textbf{Touch is sparse in time.}} \bl{It carries information only during contact.}
\dg{Averaged over a demonstration it can look unimportant while being decisive at a
few moments.} \dg{The earliest learned policy we found that shows this is See, Hear,
and Feel, which fuses a camera, a contact microphone and a GelSight sensor by
self-attention: its attention moves to touch once a peg is partly inserted, and
back to vision when the arm moves freely again \citep{li2022seehearfeel}.
3D-ViTac likewise finds touch irrelevant to the contact-free stages of its tasks
\citep{huang2025vitac}, and MS-Bot names the problem: the importance of each sense
changes from one stage of a task to the next \citep{feng2024msbot}.} \bl{A model given touch at every step must learn to
ignore it most of the time without learning to ignore it altogether.} \dg{Three
designs tackle this directly.} \dg{MS-Bot predicts the current stage from the
observations and the past actions and uses it to query the features of each
sense by cross-attention, so that each sense's weight follows the stage; it
inserted a keyed peg in 8 of 10 trials, against 6 for See, Hear, and Feel's
fusion and 5 for concatenation \citep{feng2024msbot}.} \dg{TacVLA passes its tactile
tokens only while contact is detected \citep{zhang2026tacvla}, and Dream-Tac biases
attention towards the fingertip tokens in proportion to how much each fingertip
image has just changed \citep{lou2026dreamtac} (Section~\ref{sec:rw-families}).}

\bl{\textbf{The representation decides whether touch helps.}} \bl{The same readings can
help or hurt depending on how they are presented. 3D-ViTac places each taxel at
its 3D position inside the scene's point cloud; when the same readings were
instead fed as a flat tactile image through a separate image encoder, the hex-key
task scored 0.50, below the 0.65 of using no touch at all, against 0.90 for the
point-cloud form \citep{huang2025vitac}.} \bl{Pretrained tactile encoders do not settle
the question either.} \bl{Sparsh pretrains encoders on more than 460\,000 unlabelled
images from three gel sensors and, frozen, they beat task-specific models on five
perception benchmarks; on the one control task, a bead maze learned by
behaviour cloning from frozen features, the policies completed the maze only
partially before compounding error dropped the bead, and the authors conclude
that how to use pretrained touch representations in behaviour cloning needs
further research \citep{higuera2025sparsh}.}

\bl{\textbf{Sensors differ.}} \bl{Illumination, markers and gel geometry vary between
devices, so a model trained on one sensor does not transfer to another.} \bl{This is
why Sparsh pretrains across three gel sensors and subtracts each sensor's
background frame \citep{higuera2025sparsh}.} \bl{Even sensors of one design differ in
use: on our rig the four gels have worn differently and sit at slightly
different angles, so the four fingertip images look different at rest
(Section~\ref{sec:rw-sensors}).}

\bl{\textbf{Touch is fast, cameras are slow.}} \bl{A gel sensor filmed at 25--30 frames
per second captures the shape of a contact well and its dynamics badly: the
onset of an impact, the chatter of a slip, the scrape of a texture and the
moment a plug seats are vibrations shorter than a frame.} \bl{These events are often
the first sign that a grasp is failing, which is why Sparsh-X adds contact audio,
an accelerometer and pressure to the tactile image \citep{higuera2025sparshx},
PolyTouch adds acoustic sensing \citep{zhao2025polytouch}, and Reactive Diffusion
Policy and OmniVTA run a fast tactile loop beside a slow planner
\citep{xue2025rdp,zheng2026omnivta}.}

\bl{Optical sensors add artefacts of their own.} \bl{Their illumination is not uniform,
and it is least uniform at the gel's boundary: without diffusers the boundary of
the GelSight Wedge is not lit at all \citep{wang2021wedge}, and GelSlim's
calibration warps each image and crops its boundary away \citep{donlon2018gelslim}.}
\bl{We found no work that names the bright rim our sensors show
(Section~\ref{sec:crop}), but cropping the boundary is standard practice in these
calibration pipelines, which is the step our crop experiments test.}

\subsection{\bl{How the three model families use touch}}
\label{sec:rw-families}

\bl{\textbf{Behaviour cloning.}} \bl{Most work adds touch as one more input to a
diffusion or transformer policy, and the main design question is how to fuse it
with vision.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \bl{\textbf{3D-ViTac} fuses by geometry rather than by a network.} \bl{Each taxel
  is placed at its 3D position, computed from the gripper's joint states by
  forward kinematics, inside the point cloud built from the depth cameras; a flag
  marks each point as visual or tactile, and a diffusion policy with a
  PointNet++ encoder denoises actions from the combined cloud
  \citep{huang2025vitac}.}
  \item \bl{\textbf{ViTacFormer}, built on ACT's conditional VAE, encodes four
  camera views and the fingertips' force and torque histories, and fuses them by
  cross-attention in both directions: tactile features query the visual ones,
  and visual features query the tactile ones \citep{heng2026vitacformer}.} \bl{It
  first predicts the next tactile tokens and then feeds them back as inputs to
  the action decoder.} \bl{The authors' reason is that a change in contact force
  arrives just before the event it signals, such as a peg nearing its hole, so a
  policy that forecasts touch reacts earlier; removing the forecast cut success
  on every task, for example from 100 to 70\,\% on peg insertion.}
  \item \bl{\textbf{Touch in the Wild} records a wrist fisheye camera and two
  $12\times32$ piezoresistive pads on a portable gripper.} \bl{A CLIP-initialised ViT
  encodes the image, a small CNN the tactile map, and two rounds of
  cross-attention between the two are concatenated into one embedding.} \bl{The
  encoder is pretrained by reconstructing masked tactile patches from both
  modalities, on 2.6 million visuo-tactile pairs, and then conditions a
  diffusion policy \citep{zhu2025touchwild}.}
  \item \dg{\textbf{PolyTouch}'s fingertip camera films the gel's imprint and, through a
  side window, the scene beyond it, and a contact microphone in the same finger
  records vibration \citep{zhao2025polytouch}.} \dg{Its policy encodes each fingertip
  image with a pretrained tactile encoder (T3), the microphone's log-mel
  spectrogram with an audio transformer, the wrist and scene cameras with CLIP,
  and the proprioception with an MLP.} \dg{Six cross-attention blocks fuse the
  fingertip and scene features; the rest are pooled and concatenated, and a
  diffusion head predicts the actions.} \dg{On four two-armed tasks it raised the
  average success over the same policy without the fingertip images and audio by
  3 to 34 points; on serving an egg with a spatula, the inputs concatenated without
  cross-attention reached 73\,\% and with it 100\,\%, against 66\,\% without them.}
  \item \bl{\textbf{TacThru-UMI} uses a transparent gel through which its camera
  sees both the contact and the scene beyond.} \bl{The wrist image (DINOv2 ViT-B),
  the sensor image (DINOv2 ViT-S), the tracked marker displacements and the
  proprioception (MLPs) each become tokens with a learned modality tag; the
  tokens are concatenated and a transformer diffusion policy attends over them
  all, with no separate cross-attention \citep{li2026tacthru}.}
  \item \bl{\textbf{Reactive Diffusion Policy} changes how fast touch acts rather
  than how it is encoded \citep{xue2025rdp}.} \bl{Executing an action chunk open loop
  means a policy cannot react to a contact change halfway through it, and the
  usual fixes fail: shorter chunks made the baseline's grasps fail (100 to
  20\,\%), and temporal ensembling is very sensitive to its weight.} \bl{A slow
  diffusion policy therefore plans a latent action chunk at 1--2\,Hz from the
  images, the tactile reading and the proprioception, and a fast decoder turns
  it into actions at about 24\,Hz, reading only the latest tactile signal (15
  principal components of the gel's marker motion, or the arm's estimated
  wrench).} \bl{Vision shapes the plan; touch corrects its execution.}
  \item \bl{\textbf{FARM} makes force an output rather than only an input: the
  policy predicts a target grip force alongside the arm pose and a controller
  tracks it \citep{helmut2026farm}.} \dg{Its demonstrations are recorded with a
  hand-held gripper carrying a GelSight Mini, and a pretrained finite-element
  model turns each tactile image into maps of shear and normal force, which the
  policy reads as a three-channel $96\times96$ image beside the total normal
  force.} \dg{Separate ResNet-18 encoders read the wrist camera and this force image,
  and a diffusion policy predicts the gripper pose, the grip width and the target
  grip force together.} \dg{On the robot, the gripper follows the predicted width until
  both the target and the measured force exceed 0.5\,N, and then tracks the target
  force in closed loop.} \dg{Over 20 trials per task, FARM matched a policy given only
  the total force at planting a plant and picking grapes (95\,\% each), and at
  tightening a screw with an Allen key it succeeded every time, against 10\,\% with
  the total force, 40\,\% with the raw tactile image and none with vision alone.}
\end{itemize}

\bl{\textbf{Vision-language-action models.}} \bl{Pretrained VLAs have no tactile input,
and no tactile dataset is large enough to retrain them.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \bl{\textbf{VLA-Touch} leaves its VLA (RDT-1B, reading the cameras and the
  proprioception) unchanged and adds touch at two levels \citep{bi2026vlatouch}.}
  \bl{For planning, GPT-4o receives the goal and the scene image; after each step a
  tactile-language model, Octopi, turns a few GelSight frames into a text
  description of roughness and hardness, which GPT-4o uses to choose the next
  instruction.} \bl{For control, a diffusion model refines each action chunk the VLA
  proposes, conditioned on DINOv2 image features, the proprioception and the
  shear force measured from the gel's markers, at 8\,Hz.} \bl{Removing either level
  lowered success on every task.}
  \item \bl{\textbf{DreamTacVLA} encodes each fingertip image with a frozen V-JEPA2
  encoder and a small adapter into one tactile token, concatenated with the
  camera, language and state tokens \citep{ye2025dreamtacvla}.} \bl{A contrastive loss
  aligns the tactile token with the image patches where the sensor appears.} \bl{It
  then acts in three passes: it drafts an action, predicts from the draft the
  tactile latent that will follow, and acts again conditioned on that imagined
  contact.}
  \item \bl{\textbf{TacVLA} follows pi0.5's design but opens a gate for its 36 tactile
  tokens only while contact is detected, so that touch cannot interfere during
  contact-free motion \citep{zhang2026tacvla}.}
\end{itemize}
\bl{Our pi0.5 takes the simplest route, feeding two fingertip images into image
slots pretrained on wrist cameras (Section~\ref{sec:models}).}

\textbf{World action models.} \bl{\textbf{Dream-Tac} is the only world action model
with a tactile channel that we found \citep{lou2026dreamtac}.} \bl{It finetunes a
pretrained video diffusion transformer (Cosmos-Predict2, 2\,B parameters) and
encodes the overhead, wrist and two fingertip images with the same video
autoencoder, so touch enters as further latent frames; it denoises future
images, future tactile frames and a 20-step action chunk together.} \bl{Its fusion is
gated by contact: how much each fingertip image changed since the previous frame
sets an attention bias that makes the non-tactile tokens attend more to the
tactile ones during contact.} \bl{Touch raised its average success from 52 to 74\,\%
and the gate to 83\,\%.} \bl{DreamZero and FastWAM, which we use, have no tactile
channel of their own: we tile the fingertip images into their video frame
(Section~\ref{sec:models}), so they predict touch only as pixels of that frame.}
\end{gntext}
