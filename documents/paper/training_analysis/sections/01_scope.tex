\section{Scope and question}
\label{sec:scope}

This section states the observation that prompted the comparison, defines what
cropping does to a tactile image, and names the three policy families under
test. Section~\ref{sec:recordings} then describes the recordings and how they
are divided, and Section~\ref{sec:results} reports what each policy achieves on
recordings it never saw.

The rig carries five cameras. One looks down on the workspace; the other four
are fingertip tactile sensors, one on each side of each gripper. A tactile
sensor of this kind images a deformable gel from behind, so contact appears as
a change in the gel's shading. Gradient attribution computed on training
recordings placed weight along the edges of those tactile images, and did so
before the gripper touched anything. Light entering at the boundary of the gel
would produce exactly that signature, and contact would not. Two remedies
follow from that reading: restrict the image to its centre, or admit the
tactile stream only once contact is detected. This report evaluates the first.
The second is not implemented and nothing here argues for or against it.

Cropping keeps the central four fifths of the rows of each tactile image and
discards the rest. The result is then resized back to the original dimensions,
so the shape the model receives never changes and a cropped policy can keep
every hyperparameter of its uncropped twin, including the batch size and the
number of optimisation steps. Holding the budget fixed is what makes the pair a
comparison rather than two separate runs. The resize is not isotropic: it
stretches the surviving rows vertically while leaving the columns alone.
Removing the rim and stretching the remainder therefore travel together, and
Section~\ref{sec:threats} treats that as the confound it is.

Three policy families are compared, each against its cropped twin. They are not
interchangeable, and three differences matter for how their rows may be read.
They read different numbers of cameras, because one family finetunes a
pretrained model with a fixed number of image slots and the rig has more
cameras than slots. They plan different distances ahead, which
Section~\ref{sec:results} shows is not a detail but the single largest
influence on how the comparison reads. And the attribution methods that apply
to them differ, because one family carries no spatial feature map for a
gradient method to attribute to at all.
