\section{Two world models}
\label{sec:worldmodels}

Every policy so far maps an observation to a plan of actions and nothing else.
The two models in this section also predict what the cameras will see next, so
they can be asked a question no earlier arm can: does the model know how the
scene will change? Section~\ref{sec:wmactions} first places their action error
beside the policies', and Section~\ref{sec:prediction} then scores the
predicted observations themselves.

The two were chosen to bracket one question. The first is a small world model
of about sixty million parameters, trained from scratch on this rig's
recordings alone, which reads all five cameras tiled into one frame. The second is roughly a hundred times larger and starts from
a video generator pretrained on general footage, so it arrives already knowing
how images tend to move. It reads the overhead camera and a single composite
of the four fingertip images, arranged two by two. If the pretrained prior
matters at the scale of one rig and one task, the larger model should predict
better; if the recordings carry most of what there is to learn, the gap should
be small.

Both models are scored under the treatment the policies received. The same
seven recordings at the end of the session are held back and never trained on,
every sampler is pinned to one seed so that a number repeats, and action error
is measured against the same recorded chunks. Their action rows can therefore
be read against the earlier table rather than merely beside it. The larger
model trained for fewer steps than the smaller one, because each of its steps
costs far more; \unjustified{} the step counts were set by the compute
available, not matched, so neither model is claimed to have converged.

\subsection{Action error}
\label{sec:wmactions}

Action error comes first because it is the measure the rest of this report
already rests on, and it answers whether predicting the future costs a model
anything at planning its actions.

The horizon problem of Section~\ref{sec:results} returns in a sharper form.
The large world model plans only ten steps ahead, so ten is the one horizon
every model shares, and Table~\ref{tab:worldactions} rescores every family
over its first ten steps. Only the uncropped arm of each policy family appears,
since the crop is not the question here.

\textbf{Both world models plan the next third of a second better on unseen
recordings than any of the three policies.} Their held-out errors, \num{3.9}
and \num{4.2}, sit about a fifth below the best policy's \num{5.0} and about
half the other two policies'. The two world models are within a few per cent
of each other, which is too close to rank on this evidence.

The small world model also fits its training recordings more tightly than any
other model in this report, and so has the widest gap between the two halves.
It learns the recordings it sees very closely, and it still generalises better
than the policies do; the two are not in conflict, because a gap is a ratio and
its held-out error is low in absolute terms.

This does not establish that predicting the future is what helps. The small
world model sees two past frames reaching back \SI{1.6}{\second}, where
every policy sees a single frame, and the large one starts from a pretrained video model. Either
could account for the advantage, and the comparison covers a third of a
second only.

The advantage is not the sampler's doing, at least for the small world model.
Table~\ref{tab:floor} rescores the held-back recordings under three seeds.
Over the first ten steps the small world model's error moves by about a
twentieth of a unit, against a lead of nearly a whole unit over the diffusion
family, whose own spread is smaller still. The fine-tuned policy is the
outlier: its near-term error swings by a fifth between seeds, and it is last
on every one of them. The large world model's error moves by about a twentieth
between seeds and is below the small model's on every seed tried. That is still
not a ranking: the two were scored on different samples of the held-back
frames, the large model on fewer of them.
