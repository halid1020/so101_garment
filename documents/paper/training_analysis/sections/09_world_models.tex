\section{Two world models}
\label{sec:worldmodels}

Every policy so far maps an observation to a plan of actions and nothing else.
The two models in this section also predict what the cameras will see next, so
they can be asked a question no earlier arm can: does the model know how the
scene will change? Section~\ref{sec:wmactions} first places their action error
beside the policies', and Section~\ref{sec:prediction} then scores the
predicted observations themselves.

The two were chosen to bracket one question. The first is a small world model
of about sixty million parameters, trained from scratch on this rig's
recordings alone. The second is roughly a hundred times larger and starts from
a video generator pretrained on general footage, so it arrives already knowing
how images tend to move. It reads the overhead camera and a single composite
of the four fingertip images, arranged two by two. If the pretrained prior
matters at the scale of one rig and one task, the larger model should predict
better; if the recordings carry most of what there is to learn, the gap should
be small.

Both models are scored under the treatment the policies received. The same
seven recordings at the end of the session are held back and never trained on,
every sampler is pinned to one seed so that a number repeats, and action error
is measured against the same recorded chunks. Their action rows can therefore
be read against the earlier table rather than merely beside it. The larger
model trained for fewer steps than the smaller one, because each of its steps
costs far more; \unjustified{} the step counts were set by the compute
available, not matched, so neither model is claimed to have converged.
