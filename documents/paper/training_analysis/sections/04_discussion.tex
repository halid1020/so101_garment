\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{What we learned.} The largest effect in this report is not a difference
between models. It is the drop from training to unseen demonstrations, which
every model shows and which is not caused by drift over the session. \bl{\rev{Nor is
it caused by the test demonstrations looking unfamiliar, and it is built late in
training: for all but FastWAM, test error levels off early while training error
keeps falling.}}
Fifty-eight demonstrations of one task from one session are not enough for any
of these models to generalise to a fifty-ninth.

Cropping the tactile images is not the lever. \bl{It changes almost nothing on any
model, and \rev{no model} over-weights the image edges that motivated it.}
\bl{\gn{How a pretrained model is finetuned matters more than for how long: through
a narrow LoRA pi0.5 planned worse than a small flow-matching policy trained from
scratch, and three times more training did not close the gap, but adapters on
every attention and feed-forward layer, at a batch of eight, brought it close
to the best models (Section~\ref{sec:pi05}).}} \dg{The same holds for DinoViTacFormer: with its
DINOv2 frozen it planned the far end of the plan worse than ACT, and finetuned
it matched ACT over the whole plan, though not over the first ten steps.}

\bl{Touch is used, but briefly,} \new{\bl{and removing the fingertips altogether made
ACT slightly better on held-out demonstrations.} \bl{On a task this consistent, the
overhead camera and the joint positions may already say what the next commands
should be.}} \bl{The two world action models plan the near future best, though
DreamZero barely reads its cameras to do so.} \dg{Most models were trained once.} \dg{Retraining DinoViTacFormer
without cross-attention from a second random seed moved its test error by up to 2.4 over the plan,
more than many of the differences between models in this report, so a small
difference between two single runs should be read as unconfirmed.}

\textbf{Next steps.} \bl{\textbf{Collect more varied demonstrations} (sessions,
garments, starting positions), the most direct attack on the generalisation
gap.} \bl{\textbf{Test on the robot}, since agreement with recorded commands is not
folding the garment.} \bl{\textbf{Score motion, not whole frames}, which frame-averaged PSNR ignores.}
\bl{\gn{Section~\ref{sec:future} proposes two longer-term directions.}}

\gn{\section{\bl{Future work}}
\label{sec:future}

\bl{\textbf{Fusing touch according to the grasp.}} \bl{Our results suggest that naive
tactile input is either ignored or harmful for most of a demonstration: touch
matters around contact and is noise elsewhere (Section~\ref{sec:sensors}).} \bl{The
gripper itself says when touch should matter.} \bl{When it is wide open, the
fingertips touch nothing and their images should carry little weight; when it
is closing, or holding the cloth, they carry the state the overhead camera
cannot see, such as whether one layer or two has been pinched.} \bl{We therefore
propose to weight the tactile tokens by a gate computed from the gripper's
commanded and measured opening, and from how much each fingertip image has
changed since the previous frame.} \bl{Related designs exist.} \bl{Visuotactile-RL blocks
tactile features, and their gradients, when no contact is detected
\citep{hansen2022vtrl}; TacVLA switches its tactile tokens on only during contact
\citep{zhang2026tacvla}; Dream-Tac biases attention towards the tactile tokens in
proportion to how much the fingertip images change \citep{lou2026dreamtac}; and
ReTac-ACT gates its fusion with a network conditioned on proprioception
\citep{ruan2026retacact}.} \bl{None of these gates on the grasp of a parallel gripper
folding cloth, where the moment of contact, the closing of the gripper, is known
from the robot's own state before the tactile image changes.}

\bl{\textbf{A more durable and consistent tactile sensor.}} \bl{The gel sensors wear and
see differently from finger to finger (Section~\ref{sec:rw-sensors}).} \bl{We plan to
replace the gel with a frosted acrylic sheet filmed from behind, which can be
made identically for every finger, and to repeat the comparison of
Section~\ref{sec:sensors} with it.}

\bl{\textbf{More history.}} \bl{No model here sees more than two frames
(Table~\ref{tab:models}), yet whether a fold is half done, or a grasp has
slipped, is easier to tell from a short history than from one frame.} \bl{Giving the
policies a longer window of past frames and tactile readings is a direct test
of whether this limits them.}}
