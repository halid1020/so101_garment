\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{What we learned.} The largest effect in this report is not a difference
between models. It is the drop from training to unseen demonstrations, which
every model shows and which is not caused by drift over the session. ACT shows
it most clearly: it replays familiar demonstrations almost exactly and does
much worse on new ones. Fifty-eight demonstrations of one task from one session
are not enough for any of these models to generalise to a fifty-ninth.

Cropping the tactile images is not the lever. It changes almost nothing on any
model, and on average the policies do not over-weight the image edges that
motivated it. The edges are brighter than the centre, but the policies'
behaviour does not depend on them in a way we can measure.

\new{Pretraining did not help as we used it. pi0.5, finetuned through a narrow
LoRA, plans worse than a small flow-matching policy with the same objective
trained from scratch on our 58 demonstrations. Training three times longer
did not close the gap, which points at the narrow LoRA rather than too little
training.}

Touch is used, but briefly. ACT ignores the fingertips for seconds at a time
and then leans on them heavily around contact, so averaged contributions
understate what the tactile sensors do. \new{Yet removing the fingertips
altogether made ACT slightly better on held-out demonstrations. On a task this
consistent, with a fixed garment and a fixed folding strategy, the overhead
camera and the joint positions may already say what the next commands should
be; the tactile sensors may matter more on tasks whose outcome depends on how
firmly the cloth is held.}

The two world action models plan the near future best, and DreamZero predicts the
cameras better than assuming nothing moves. FastWAM predicts sharper frames,
but only a second ahead, and frame-averaged scores undersell it.

\textbf{Next steps.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Collect more varied demonstrations} (sessions, garments,
  starting positions): the most direct attack on the generalisation gap.
  \item \textbf{Test on the robot.} Agreement with recorded commands is not
  folding the garment, nor recovering from states the demonstrator never
  visited.
  \item \new{\textbf{Finetune pi0.5 fully, or with LoRA on every layer,} as
  the published recipes do; this needs a larger GPU.}
  \item \textbf{Gate touch on contact,} which fits how ACT uses the fingertips
  and removes the pre-contact images that raised the edge question.
  \item \textbf{Predict further, and score motion.} Train FastWAM to predict
  beyond one second, and score predictions on the pixels that change, which
  frame-averaged PSNR ignores.
\end{enumerate}
